Questions before we build the scaled platform

NYU Urban Systems Lab
La Maraña · Rebuild by Design
Prepared 14 Sep 2026

La Maraña's 100-question test set is the clearest statement of the product we have. Read as a specification rather than a test, it describes something materially larger than what we have built: a spatial analysis platform with a conversational interface, not a document assistant with a map beside it.

Separately, La Maraña is sending a 600-layer geodatabase and reconstructed metadata. Both arrive at the same moment. Before we commit an architecture to carry them, these are the questions whose answers change what we build.

Each question below states why it matters and, where useful, the options we see. Sections are marked for the audience they need to reach.

Balanced by design: 25 questions at each specificity level S1–S4, 10 in each of 10 domains. Five of the ten domains have no data loaded at all — Water, Environment, Demographics, Agriculture and Cadastre — which makes 50 of the 100 unanswerable on data grounds regardless of engine capability.

01Scope — what are we actually building?Both

The gap between the test set and the current build is not a backlog; it is a different category of system. This needs to be said out loud and agreed before estimates mean anything.

1.1

Is the 100-question set the product specification, or an aspirational ceiling?

This is the single highest-leverage question in the document. If it is the spec, we are building a spatial analysis engine and the roadmap roughly triples. If it is aspirational, we need to know which subset is the committed deliverable for the current scope of work (through April 2027).

Options as we see them
  • Full set — commit to spatial analysis, map selection and uploads. Largest scope.
  • S1–S2 only (50 questions) — thematic and municipality-level. No drawing, no uploads. Closest to today.
  • Depth-first by domain — do 2–3 domains completely, including S3/S4, and stage the rest.
1.2

Which of the five empty domains are in scope, and who supplies the data?

Water, Environment, Demographics, Agriculture and Cadastre have no data loaded. The 600-layer GDB likely covers water, environment and agriculture. Census demographics and CRIM cadastre are probably separate acquisitions with their own licensing and privacy questions — Q081 explicitly limits cadastre to "non-personal information", which implies a data-handling policy we have not written.

1.3

Is document upload in scope for this phase? Affects privacy review

Thirteen questions have a user upload their own plan, study or EIS. That introduces user-generated content into a system whose entire trust claim is "only La Maraña's verified data" — and it reopens the privacy, retention and security questions currently in front of NYU IT. It is also the feature most likely to require IRB consideration if uploads are studied.

1.4

Does the platform need map export and download?

Q023, Q033 and Q043 all ask to produce a map for a presentation, class or community use. That is a publishing feature — layout, attribution, licensing of derived products — not a minor addition to the viewer.

02Data & the 600 layersLa Maraña

These determine whether the incoming geodatabase can be ingested automatically or layer-by-layer by hand. At 600 layers the difference is weeks.

2.1

Do the reconstructed metadata sheets share consistent column headers?

If every sheet uses the same fields, we load all 600 with one script. If each sheet differs, it becomes manual work multiplied by 600. The single most cost-relevant question we can ask right now.

Fields most useful to us, in priority order
  • Layer name · source agency · vintage year · category · short description
  • Then: license or use restrictions, methodology note, update frequency
  • And explicitly: "unknown" recorded as unknown, never inferred — which is what you have already done
2.2

How should layers with missing metadata be presented to users?

Some layers will have gaps. We would rather show "source not documented" than guess — consistent with the standard set after the FEMA placeholder. But should such layers be published with a warning, held back, or shown only to planners? This is a policy call, not a technical one.

2.3

Which of the 600 layers matter most?

We can ingest all of them, but we cannot style, translate, categorize and validate 600 with equal care at once. A ranked first tranche — the 40–60 layers the test questions actually need — lets us deliver working answers sooner.

2.4

Are Census demographics and CRIM cadastre available to this project?

Twenty questions depend on them. If they are not obtainable, those two domains should be removed from the test set so we are not measured against data we cannot have.

2.5

How often does each layer change, and who republishes it?

Determines whether ingestion is one-off or a standing pipeline — and after handoff, whether La Maraña can refresh a layer without engineering support. This shapes the operational design more than any other data question.

03Users & accessBoth

The test set names fifteen distinct personas, from residents to government agencies. They do not want the same product.

3.1

Who is the primary user — and who are we willing to serve badly?

A resident asking "can I build here?" and an engineering firm comparing three road corridors need different interfaces. Designing for both without saying which leads first produces a tool that serves neither. Our reading is that the community leader and resident lead, and the planner is served second — worth confirming.

3.2

Do users need accounts? Contradiction on record

We told NYU IT that login is deferred because persistent accounts are not feasible for La Maraña to manage long-term. The design scope lists accounts, saved sessions and tier-gating as P0. Both cannot be true. Saved areas and history are genuinely useful, but they commit someone to operating an identity system after handoff.

3.3

Is there a partner or API tier?

If municipalities or agencies will query programmatically, that is a contract, a rate limit and a support obligation. It changes the architecture now rather than later.

3.4

What is the answer on public access? Blocking launch

NYU policy still blocks anonymous access. Org-policy exception or the ClimateIQ pattern — either is workable, but nothing public ships until one is chosen. Cynthia's CAPTCHA point is the right instinct and is in the plan either way.

04Architecture & scalingInternal

Decisions we should settle among ourselves before committing to the partner.

4.1

Do we accept vector tiles as the map delivery model?

Whole-layer GeoJSON already fails at ten layers: one land-use layer is 26.4 MB, and the landslide layer is silently truncated to 6,000 of 53,935 features — users see 11% of a hazard layer with no warning. Tiles fix both. We consider this settled unless someone disagrees, but it should be said aloud because everything downstream assumes it.

4.2

Do we build a spatial query engine, and how far does it go?

The largest single decision. 93 of 100 questions need it. There is a real ladder here and we should pick a rung deliberately.

Capability ladder
  • Tier 1 — point-in-polygon, counts within an area, straight-line distance. Mostly exists.
  • Tier 2 — buffers ("within 500 m"), multi-layer overlay, percentage of a parcel in a zone.
  • Tier 3 — multi-criteria scoring and ranking (Q020, Q058, Q068, Q088).
  • Tier 4 — network analysis: travel time, evacuation routing (Q042, Q049).
4.3

How does a natural-language question become a spatial query, safely?

"Schools within 500 m of a river in Arecibo" must become real PostGIS. Letting a model emit SQL against the database is the fast path and the dangerous one. Our recommendation is a constrained tool layer — a fixed set of parameterized spatial operations the model may call, never free-form SQL. Slower to build, but auditable, and every result stays explainable and citable.

4.4

Is a computed result allowed to be an answer?

Today every claim traces to a document or an official layer. A computed distance or a ranked comparison is a new kind of claim the system itself generates. The confidence taxonomy handles this by labelling it "calculated" with its method stated — but La Maraña should confirm that a system-generated number, clearly marked, is acceptable given the concerns they raised about sourcing.

4.5

What is the performance target, and at what concurrency?

"Thousands of questions" needs a number. Answer latency, tile latency, and expected peak concurrent users determine caching strategy, replica count and budget. A public launch tied to a community event behaves very differently from steady traffic.

4.6

What must La Maraña be able to do without engineers after handoff?

Add a layer? Correct metadata? Retire a dataset? Review flagged answers? Each answered "yes" is an admin interface that has to be designed and built. This is the question most likely to be discovered too late, and it should shape the build from here rather than be retrofitted in the final months.

5.1

Is SIGE PR the benchmark?

Nias linked the SIGE viewers twice. If that is the bar for map capability, it sets a clear and useful target — and it tells us the audience expects a professional GIS viewer, with Mappealo's contribution being the conversational and provenance layer on top rather than the map alone.

5.2

Who owns Spanish-language quality?

Register and tone matter more than literal translation, particularly for uncertainty and disclaimer text. We would like a standing review loop with La Maraña for all user-facing copy — but it needs a named owner and a cadence to be real.
